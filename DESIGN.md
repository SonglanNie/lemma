## 3.0: Lemma's own look, for a public release

For the first public release Lemma keeps the VS Code-style workbench and gains an identity of its own, with the choice of look offered in the tour. Lemma now looks like itself before a theme is chosen, and every VS Code theme still dresses it.

- **Lemma Dark and Lemma Light** are the defaults for new readers and follow macOS light and dark. ## 2.4: the Library as an editor's tree

The Library is one flattened tree of 22 pt rows (24 pt in Classic): a 10 pt indent per level, a 16 pt twistie, a 16 pt icon, then the name. A new library's name field appears where the library will be.

## 0.9: the structure of a mathematical text

0.9 set out to serve students and researchers beyond a single textbook. Research on readers' complaints (Hacker News, Math StackExchange, MathOverflow, Zotero forums, ScholarPhi and CiteSee studies) pointed to three gaps: notation kept by hand, no view of the results without their proofs, and no way to see what a result rests on. 0.9 answers them with an editor's own tools, symbol outline, code folding, call hierarchy, a dependency graph and hovers, in the same native vocabulary.

- **Kind badges:** statements carry a 15 pt rounded square with a letter, coloured by kind as an editor colours symbol kinds: D definition (blue), T theorem (purple), L lemma (teal), P proposition (indigo), C corollary (pink), E example (orange); equations teal E, figures orange F, sections grey §. The same badge appears in Results, the Hierarchy panel and the map.
- **Results view** (activity bar, `checkmark.seal`): filter field, chips (All, Definitions, Results, Examples), a progress ring with “12 of 48 understood” and a Fold Proofs button, then statements by chapter. A row is badge, “Theorem 2.3” (semibold when on the page in view), the title in secondary text, the number of users in tertiary, a proof fold square (olive when folded) and an understood circle (green when checked).
- **Folded proofs:** a fold bar covers each of the proof's columns on each page: paper `#FBFAF5` with a dashed olive hairline and 4 pt corners, a bold olive chevron and “Proof of Theorem 2.3 · folded”, then “12 lines, try it yourself, then click to unfold” in grey (beside the title when the bar is one line tall, below it otherwise). Continuations on later pages read “Proof continued · folded”. Hover darkens the border; a click unfolds. Colours are fixed because the page is white paper in both appearances.
- **Gutter:** left of the page (or inside its left margin when the window is narrow), a 16 pt fold chevron at each proof's first line (grey, olive and filled when folded) and a green `checkmark.seal.fill` beside understood statements, an editor's folding and test-status gutter.
- **Hierarchy panel tab:** a 30 pt header with the badge, title, page, a segmented Uses / Used By control with counts, a map button and a “statement in view” button; rows of 24 pt with a disclosure chevron, badge, title, an “in proof” capsule, ×count and page, indented 16 pt per level, cycles not expanded.
- **Dependency map** (side slot, like the Lab): layers from what rests on nothing (top) to what rests on most; nodes 176 × 42 pt on control background with a 7 pt radius, kind badge, label and title; edges are curves from the used node's bottom to the user's top with a small arrowhead, thicker with more mentions. Hovering a node highlights its edges in olive and dims the rest; the node on the page in view has an olive border, understood nodes a green one. A legend strip explains the arrows.
- **Reading ruler:** the line under the ruler keeps full contrast with a faint olive wash and a 3 pt olive notch at its left; every visible page around it is veiled with 58 % white paper. A strip under the breadcrumbs (olive tint, like the selection bar) shows “Line 12 of 38 · p. 52”, the statement being read, the keys, and Why? / Uses / close; the status bar shows an olive “Ruler · line 12/38” item.
- **Symbol hover:** a 320 pt popover: the symbol in 20 pt italic serif beside its meaning (12.5 pt medium) and “Defined · p. 63” (or “Named”, or “Defined later”), then up to four other meanings with their pages. The page inspector's **Notation** section lists the page's symbols the same way (15 pt italic serif symbol, meaning, page).
- **Sticky statement header:** a 24 pt strip on regular material across the top of the page, shown only while the head of the statement being read is off screen and the ruler is off: a chevron (or the proof glyph inside a proof), the kind badge, “Proof of Theorem 3.2” in 11.5 pt semibold, the title in secondary text and “from p. 51” in tertiary monospaced digits. A click goes back to the statement; right-click has the statement menu. The breadcrumb's last crumb names the same statement.
- **“?” marks:** a mark is a question note titled “? …” with the line's crop and no question yet. The status bar shows a “?” item with the open count; ⇧⌘/ flashes the first mark. Marks keep the reading position: nothing opens when one is made.
- **Look Up across the library:** below the book's own sections, “In your other books” (caption, `books.vertical`) lists up to six hits from the other books' analyses, the book's name in semibold, the term in italic, a “definition” capsule when the book defines it there, and the passage or index pages on one secondary line. A click opens that book's tab at the page.

## 1.0: derivations, editor groups, diagnostics and the page as an editor

- **Derivation editor** (a side tab, `function` icon): a 28 pt header (olive ƒ, title, double-click to rename -, page, a verdict capsule “✓ 3 ✗ 1” in green and red on a faint fill, a menu: Rename, Copy as LaTeX, Export as Markdown, Insert Declarations from the Book, Delete; close). Opening the same thing again brings its tab forward; one map at a time; at most eight tabs. The inspector steps aside for a notebook or a derivation in a laptop-sized window and comes back when the last of them closes.
- **Problems panel** (first tab of the bottom panel): groups with disclosure chevrons, Failing steps (red `xmark.circle.fill`), Passages marked “?” (orange `questionmark.circle.fill`), In progress (blue `info.circle.fill`), each row a title, its location in tertiary digits and, below, the detail in small monospaced text. The status bar's left side shows the counts as an editor does (⊗ red when non-zero, ? orange), and a click opens the panel.
- **Line-number gutter:** 9 pt monospaced digits beside every reading line, right-aligned 24 pt left of the page (fold chevrons keep the 20 pt between), or inside the page's left margin aligned on the text column when the window leaves no room; on two-column pages the right column's numbers sit on the right. The line under the reader's eye is in label colour, the others grey. A 2.5 pt **change bar** between number and page marks a line the reader highlighted (in the highlight's colour), noted (olive), commented (ochre), marked “?” (orange) or drew on (blue). The status bar's page item reads “p. 64 · Ln 12/41 · PDF 83 of 764”.
- **Page themes:** Paper (the original), Sepia (paper multiplied by a warm beige, ink unchanged) and Night (luminance inverted with hue kept, then mapped to #1E1E1E…#DEDEDE; colours stay recognisable). Each page tile is drawn into a bitmap at the tile's resolution and recoloured; the PDF view's background becomes 0.085 grey. Marks drawn over the page follow the theme: the light olive and lighter greys on a night page, a dark veil for the ruler, a dark paper for folded proofs. Captures, crops sent to ChatGPT, hover previews and exports always render the original page.
- **Catalog** (activity bar, `square.grid.2x2`): a search field, subject chips, an arXiv field, then rows like extension listings, a 30×40 cover in the subject's colour with the title's initials in serif, the title in semibold, authors and year, a two-line summary (four on hover), a level capsule, the size, and a **Get** button in olive (a progress bar and cancel while downloading, **Open** once in the library). A footnote says where the books come from and that nothing downloads without a click.
- **Find the Paper** (in a citation's peek): a link-style button, a small spinner naming the services searched, then up to four cards: title in 12.5 pt semibold, a “Likely”/“Possible” capsule (green/orange) for how well authors, year and title match, authors · year · venue, up to four lines of abstract, and **Open in a New Tab** (olive) when a free PDF exists, Publisher ↗ or arXiv ↗, and the citation count.

## Command palette and Go to

⇧⌘P lists every command as “Group: Action” with its shortcut (Lab commands appear while a notebook is open). The selected row is a dark olive behind white text in both appearances. ⌘P goes to a printed page (“124”, or “pdf 143”), a label (“(3.41)”, “fig 3.8”, “ex 2.8”, “3.4.1”), or searches sections, numbered objects, index terms, notes, books and commands with one ranking; “>” switches to commands and “@” (⌘T) to symbols. Return goes there with Back available; ⌥Return opens it in the side editor. The palette is an opaque floating list at the top of the window, with a key hint footer.

## The book's apparatus

Lemma reads each book once in the background (about ten seconds for ESL) and caches the result: printed page numbers from running heads, every numbered definition and mention, the back-of-book index (parsed from indentation, since the text layer loses it) and the reference list with author–year or numbered citations. The analysis is a local reading aid; it does not interpret mathematics. Printed page numbers appear wherever a page is named once they are known.

## Study interaction

Capturing a region, drawing ink, and selecting ordinary text are distinct modes. A persistent strip explains how to select text or capture a region and shows passage actions disabled until a source is selected. It offers Ask, Note, Comment, Highlight (with highlight colours and underline), Pin, Look up and OCR; labels collapse to icons when the editor is narrow, while Highlight keeps its text and dropdown arrow. The VS Code strip uses the existing native-menu wrapper so a colour theme cannot replace the label with an unreadable accent. Notes in the page inspector’s Your work list and in the note editor have visible trash buttons, with native Undo; pending questions must be cancelled before deletion. Draw replaces it with Pen/Eraser, color, and width controls. Ink uses PDF coordinates and native Undo/Redo. Original/My edition controls the personal layer through the layout menu; export creates a separate PDF.

Resting the pointer on a reference for under half a second shows a hover preview, as an editor's hover: a small popover above the mention with the kind's symbol, its name, printed page and reference count, and the defining region rendered on white at up to 1.5× (at most 500 × 300 pt), or a citation's entry text. It never takes focus, closes when the pointer leaves, the page scrolls, the window loses focus or a click starts, and can be turned off in Settings.

References are the book's own hyperlinks. Recognised mentions, numbered objects, PDF links and, after the analysis, citations, carry a dotted olive underline in My edition; hovering makes it solid with a faint fill and a pointing-hand cursor. A click opens a PDF-anchored peek with a live preview of the defining region (its label highlighted), its printed page and reference count, and Find All References, Pin, Open to the Side and Go to. A citation's peek shows its entry in the reference list with Copy Reference, Search Scholar and how often the book cites it. Reference counts (“4 refs · 1 note”) sit beside equation labels and above captions, at the page's type size; clicking one opens the References panel. Right-click adds Peek, Go to, Open to the Side, Pin and Find All References above PDFKit's own items, plus the selection's study actions, or Add Margin Note Here on an unselected line.

⌘-click or Look up shows, first, where the book defines the term, “Defined in the book”, each term in italic as printed with its page, a small “definition” tag when a cue such as “called” surrounds it, and the sentence with the term in bold, then the book's index entries for the term (with page chips; ⌥-click opens a page to the side) above the reader's saved glossary. Glossary content is supplied by the reader; notation, dimensions, scope, and assumptions fields do not claim automatic analysis.

Margin notes are the pencilled remark beside a line: double-click the page's margin, choose Comment, or ⌥⌘M. A small editor opens beside the line (Markdown and LaTeX, first line as title); an empty note disappears when closed. In a wide window the note sits in the margin as a sticky-note card, aligned with its line; clicking it reopens the editor.

Quick questions open in a popover beside the selection: the passage crop, an optional question field (Return sends), help styles as capsules, the context as checkbox rows with an image count, and the sending mode. Every shared item is visible before sending; reasoning and saved explanations of glossary entries never leave the Mac. The full question sheet groups the same context under From the book, Your glossary, Linked notes and Pinned references, with thumbnails.

Exercises found by the analysis carry a status (to do, attempted, solved) in the Symbols view; Work on This Exercise opens a working note from the exercise's text and marks it attempted.

Linked notes, the dependency sheet, OCR, page search, experiments, saved explanations and Review keep their 0.6 behavior: relationships are reader-chosen, OCR is explicit and local with the source image beside the editable text, indexing is checkpointed and guarded, experiments ask for a prediction first, and Review shows due notes one at a time with cloze masks, Reveal (⌘↩) and Again/Hard/Good/Easy (⌘1–⌘4).

## States and accessibility

Use native keyboard actions, descriptive labels, semantic buttons, accessible math markup, and ordinary focus behavior. Activity-bar buttons, tabs, breadcrumbs, the command center, the overview ruler (adjustable), reference counts and the panel's resize handle have accessibility labels. Empty panes explain how to add the missing content; the Symbols and Index views show reading progress while the analysis runs and explain when a document has no index. Save failures remain visible, browser work has a pending state, and actions require an appropriate source or note.

Settings (⌘,) holds reference links and counts, the quick-question sending mode and the default context choices. Help → Keyboard Shortcuts (⌘/) lists Go to, the workbench, references, passage work, review and files.

No decorative animation is used. The PDF remains a native PDFKit accessibility surface; a visual recall mask is a reading aid, not an accessibility or content-redaction boundary.
