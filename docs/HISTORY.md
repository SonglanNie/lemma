# Lemma history

Release notes as they were written for each version before 3.0, newest first. [CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md) summarizes them; [the guide](GUIDE.md) describes how the features work today.

An IDE for reading mathematics and scientific documents on a Mac. Read the original page; its references, index and citations become links; keep earlier definitions beside you; work derivations through with every step checked; and save questions, reasoning and explanations beside their source.

Version 2.5.0 is built with SwiftUI, AppKit, PDFKit, Swift Charts, Apple Vision, Natural Language, an offline Markdown/LaTeX renderer and, for the Lab, the Python 3 already installed on the Mac. It runs on macOS 15 or later. The local build is optimized and ad-hoc signed; it is not a notarized public release.

2.5 makes **the Lemma Tour** short and puts it on your own window. The tour is now six steps, about two minutes: the Library, Go to, references as links, the selection bar, the notebook and the activity bar. Every step lights up the part of the real window it explains and points at it with a callout (what Show Me used to do on request); a part that is not on screen, such as the page before a book is open, shows the step's animation instead, and the window stays usable underneath. What the old hundred steps covered is now in nine **guides** of three or four steps: Your Window, Reading, Proofs, Checked Derivations, The Lab, Notes and Your Edition, Ask an Assistant, Remember and Research, Make It Yours. **Help › Lemma Tour** starts the tour, **Help › Lemma Guides** opens the guides (also `Help: Lemma Guides` in the command palette, the gear menu and the tour's last page). The first launch keeps the choice of interface and colours, on two pages. Closing the tour hides again the parts of the window it opened. [The tour](TUTORIAL.md).

2.4 rebuilds the **Library** as an editor's tree, in both interfaces. A click anywhere on a row opens a library, section or folder, not only its chevron, and a click on a PDF opens it; the arrow keys, Space, ⌘↓ and typing a name move through it, ⌘-click and ⇧-click select several rows, and the Library follows the book in front. **Return** renames a PDF in Lemma only, its file on disk, or both at once, with the reason shown if a name cannot be used; libraries, sections and folders rename the same way. Drag PDFs or folders onto another library to file them there (the files stay put; ⌥ keeps them in both), onto a folder to move the files on disk, libraries onto libraries to nest or reorder them, sections as a whole, **Other PDFs** onto a library to file them all, and folders from Finder onto the empty space to make new libraries. The context menu adds Move to Library, Also List In, New Library from This Folder, Library Type, Remove from Library, Open in Preview, Reveal in Finder and Move to Trash; the view's menu sorts PDFs and collapses everything. **⌘Z** undoes every Library change, including renamed and trashed files. [Organise the Library](LIBRARIES.md#organise-the-library).

2.3.2 makes **Highlight** visible above the page, with disabled passage actions and a selection hint before a source is selected. **Your work** and the note editor have a visible trash button; deleting a note supports **⌘Z** to undo.

2.3.1 uses **⌥⌘← (Back)** and **⌥⌘→ (Forward)** for reading-history navigation. Option, Command and arrow keys are directly accessible on French and US Mac keyboards; the menus, toolbar hints, command palette and tour show the same shortcuts.

2.3 adds **assistants inside Lemma**. Choose **ChatGPT Web** (the default), **Codex** or **Claude Code** when asking about a passage or a note. ChatGPT's page and sign-in are embedded in Lemma; Codex and Claude Code run their official installed CLI with its subscription login. Questions, progress, cancellation and completed answers stay in the study window. You can continue reading or switch books while Lemma saves the answer to its original source note, including its provider and available model metadata. **Assistants…** in the Study/Help menus or the activity bar manages connections and questions. [Setup and limits](ASSISTANTS.md).

2.2.1 refreshes the tour's buttons and miniature labels, improves contrast in imported themes, and gives the Python console syntax-coloured input, readable prompts and outputs, a Run button and keyboard hints. Explorer now uses ⌥⌘1, avoiding the ⇧⌘E global shortcut used by TickTick.

2.2 adds **the Lemma Tour**. On the first launch, Lemma opens a guided tour that starts with the choice between the **VS Code** and **Classic** interfaces (switched live behind the card) and your colours, then explains every feature in 17 chapters and about a hundred steps, each with a short animation drawn in your own colour theme. **Show Me** lights up the part of your real window a step is about; **Try It** runs the feature while the tour waits in a pill at the bottom. ← and → move, Escape closes, and a search field finds any step. Reopen it from **Help › Lemma Tutorial** (or one chapter from Help › Tutorial Chapters), the command palette (`Help: Lemma Tutorial`), the gear menu, or the tour card of the Welcome page and the Classic home. [The tour](TUTORIAL.md).

2.1.4 fixes **Redo after deleting a note**, restores the saved active **Settings/Welcome** tab when a book reopens, and makes exported notebook links follow their actual filenames when titles collide. Validation records the reproductions and checks.

2.1.3 adds Library drag and drop into the reader: drop a PDF near the right edge to open it beside the current one, or onto an existing side group. The same PDF can appear in both groups, with independent pages and zoom saved across restarts. Right-click → **Open to the Side** works for unopened PDFs too. [Library workflows](LIBRARIES.md#read-two-pdfs-side-by-side).

2.1.2 makes fast minimap dragging cheaper: navigation uses the latest pointer position, transient page analysis waits for release, and the final reading destination is saved. It also avoids redundant annotation updates and repeated status-bar counts.

2.1.1 makes dividers easier to grab throughout the workbench, adds remembered sizes to Structure, Run and Debug, editor groups, notes, projects, derivations and formula comparisons, and adds **Rename Library…** and **Rename Section…**. **Terms introduced** now requires a definition cue or the book's index and recognizes French definition titles. App-authored labels and exports use ordinary punctuation without em dashes.

2.1 adds **Books / Novels**, **Studying Library, Stats / Quant / School** and **Papers**, with recursive folder watching: new PDFs appear automatically. School keeps CSD, Economics and their source subfolders. **File › Manage Library Sources…** adds folders or libraries. **Catalog › arXiv Papers** searches the remote catalogue and downloads selected papers; the Chrome companion's button adds the arXiv article in the current tab. 2.0.3 fixes minimap dragging and reading-position updates, makes the Explorer sections resizable with remembered heights, and keeps side bars and the bottom panel usable in smaller windows. Double-click a separator to reset its size.

## New in 2.0: drawn as VS Code, with a debugger for proofs

Lemma's window is now laid out and drawn as VS Code's workbench, and the 1.1 window stays one setting away.

- **Interface style** (Settings › Workbench › Appearance, the Lemma menu, or the command palette): **VS Code** (the default) or **Classic (Lemma 1.1)**, the native macOS window with its toolbar, SF Symbols and System/Light/Graphite/Midnight themes. It switches at once, without a restart; every feature works in both.
- **The VS Code workbench:** a 35 pt title bar with Back/Forward, the command center (⌘P) and the layout controls; a 48 pt activity bar of codicons (Explorer, Search, Structure, Run and Debug, Testing, Lab, Projects, Marks, Catalog, Accounts, Manage); side bar views with collapsible panes; editor tabs (35 pt, close on hover, a 1 pt accent line on the active tab) with the editor's actions at the end; symbol breadcrumbs; the panel (PROBLEMS, REFERENCES, HIERARCHY, QUESTIONS, NOTES, CONSOLE, with Maximize); the secondary side bar (PAGE, NOTES, GLOSSARY, CONTEXT, REVIEW); a 22 pt status bar with a coloured mode indicator on the left; notifications at the bottom right (the bell keeps them). Sizes, fonts (the system font at 13 pt, Menlo 12 for code) and icons follow VS Code's own source.
- **Colour themes (⌥⌘T):** 84 themes, VS Code's built-in ones (Dark 2026 and Light 2026, its current defaults, Dark/Light Modern, Dark+/Light+, High Contrast, Monokai, Solarized, Abyss…), Lemma's (Midnight, Paper, Graphite) and popular community themes (Moonlight, Moonlight II, SynthWave '84, One Dark Pro, Dracula, GitHub, Tokyo Night, Catppuccin, Nord, Night Owl, Gruvbox, Rosé Pine…). The picker previews each theme as you move through it, as VS Code's does. Install any VS Code theme by dropping its `.json` or `.vsix` on the window; **Use My VS Code Theme** reads the theme you chose in VS Code. Missing colours are resolved with VS Code's own registered defaults and colour arithmetic. Automatic switching follows macOS light and dark. Midnight (the 1.1 theme that did not apply) now works in both styles.
- **Run and Debug a proof (F5):** put the reading position in a theorem (or right-click it in Results, or right-click a line → Run and Debug from Here). The reading line becomes the current line, highlighted as a debugger's stack frame. **Step over** to the next line (F10 or ↓), **step into** the result, equation or section the line cites (F11 or Return, PDF links of books made with hyperref are followed too), **step out** back to where you were (⇧F11 or Shift-Return), **continue** to the next breakpoint or to the end of the proof (F5). **Variables** shows the *goal*, the statement being proved, rendered from the page, the symbols on the current line with the meaning in force there, those introduced since the statement began, and what the proof uses; **Watch** follows symbols, labels such as (3.41) or index terms as you step; **Call Stack** shows where you stepped in; **Breakpoints** are lines you mark with F9 and your “?” marks. At the end of a proof Lemma offers to mark it understood. The status bar shows the frame being read.
- **Testing:** definitions and results as tests, by chapter, with the result of your last recall (passed, failed, due, untested), a filter (`@failed`, `@due`, `@untested`), **Run Due Tests** and **Run Tests in This Chapter**; failed tests come back sooner (the existing Review schedule).
- **Minimap:** miniatures of the pages beside the page, with the visible region as a slider and your highlights, notes, “?” marks and search matches drawn over them. Click a miniature, drag the slider or scroll over it to move. The slider follows the pointer, including in Single Page mode.
- **Search across the library:** the Search view searches the text of the book in front or of every book in your library, with Match Case and Whole Word, and lists the matches by book with the words around them; a click opens the book at the match, and the open book's matches are marked in its minimap and scroll bar. The first search reads each book's text in Lemma's helper process (so the reader's memory stays small) and keeps it beside the book's analysis; later searches take a fraction of a second. Notes and indexed pages keep their own tabs.
- **Explorer:** Open Editors, your Library, the book's **Outline** as a tree that follows your reading, a **Timeline** of your sessions, notes, answers, derivations and notebooks, and the Pages. Drag the line above an expanded section to share space with the expanded section above it; double-click to reset their heights. Heights are remembered in this library, including when sections are collapsed and reopened.
- **Workbench shortcuts:** ⇧⌘P the command palette, ⌘P Go to, ⌥⌘1 Explorer, ⇧⌘F Search, ⇧⌘D Run and Debug, ⇧⌘X Catalog, ⌘B and ⌥⌘B the side bars, ⌘J the panel, ⌘W closes the editor in front. Lemma's own moved where they clashed: Zen Mode ⌥⌘Z, Export Study Notes ⌥⇧⌘E, Draw on My Edition ⌥⇧⌘D.
- **Editors as tabs:** **Settings** (searchable, with categories and a blue bar beside the settings you changed), **Keyboard Shortcuts**, **Welcome** (Start, Recent, This Week and a *Get Started* walkthrough with theme tiles) and **What's New** open as tabs. Any book of the library can open beside the current one (right-click a tab or a book → Open to the Side).
- **VS Code's widgets:** hovers are rectangles with a hint row, a reference's peek opens as a peek view under the line (title bar, frame, actions), the command palette is VS Code's quick input (recently used commands first, key caps, matched characters in bold), notebooks and derivations take the theme's syntax colours, and errors arrive as notifications rather than alerts.

The [2.0 research notes](RESEARCH_2_0.md) record the sources: VS Code's source at a fixed commit, its theme files, and what students and researchers wrote about reading mathematics in 2024–2026.

## Earlier: new in 1.1: research across books and a personal workbench

- **Research projects:** follow a question across several books. Collect a selection with ⇧⌘C, or collect an existing note's source from its context menu. Write a Markdown/LaTeX synthesis beside the page, append citations such as S1, and follow them back into the book. Each source keeps its original crop, extracted text, printed and physical page, and a separate personal annotation. Search the project's books, archive and restore projects, and export the synthesis with its images and metadata.
- **Customize Workbench:** the title bar's Layout menu and Settings offer System, Light, Graphite and Midnight themes, four accents, Compact/Comfortable density, a left or right explorer, resizable panes, and Reading/Study/Research/Lab layouts. System appearance and olive remain the defaults; page themes are independent.
- **Resume your work:** book tabs, their order, side editors, selected notes, notebook sections and pane sizes survive book switches and restarts. Drag tabs to reorder them, or use their context menus. Restoring a Python notebook opens its editor without executing it.
- **Read Formula as LaTeX:** select a formula, or use an equation's context menu in Symbols. Compare the original with an editable local transcription and typeset preview; copy the LaTeX or start a source-linked derivation. It reads PDF glyph positions heuristically: compare and correct the result before using it. Scanned pages and unfamiliar fonts may have no usable transcription.

## Earlier: new in 1.0, derivations checked as you type

1.0 adds what an IDE does best, tell you, as you type, where you went wrong, to the place a student gets stuck most: a step the book says is “easily seen”. And it gives the window the rest of an editor's tools: editor groups with tabs, a Problems panel, line numbers, a dark page, an extensions-like catalog, and Go to Definition across papers.

- **Derivations (⌥⌘E, or Derive in the selection bar).** Select the step you do not follow, “the ridge regression solutions are easily seen to be (3.44)”, and choose Derive. A derivation opens beside the page: write one step per line in LaTeX (or paste the Unicode of a PDF or a chat answer), and Lemma checks each line **as you type**, the way a type checker underlines code. Every step is tested on random values of its symbols, numbers, vectors and matrices of consistent sizes, so a ✗ comes with a counterexample (“X 5×3, λ = 0.62 → left 2.31, right 1.87”), and the usual slips are named: *sign error*, *off by a factor of 2*, *off by a constant*, *transposed*. The gutter shows each line's verdict, failing steps are underlined, and the steps are typeset below with what Lemma found.
  - **Shapes are inferred:** Xᵀ makes X a matrix, Xβ with X of size N×p makes β a vector of length p, y − Xβ makes y one of length N; a strip above the editor shows what each symbol is (click an inferred one to write it as a declaration you can change). Declare sizes and assumptions on a line of their own: `X \in \mathbb{R}^{N\times p}`, `\lambda > 0`, `0 < p < 1`, `A \succ 0`, `x_i \in \mathbb{R}^p`. A new derivation already declares the sizes the book's own notation gives (“X: the N × p input matrix, p. 45”).
  - **The mathematics of a derivation:** sums with symbolic bounds, products, fractions, roots, norms, traces, determinants, inverses and transposes, derivatives (∂/∂β of a scalar is the gradient), definite integrals (also to ∞), factorials and binomials, arbitrary functions f, g, h (so the chain rule can be checked), and definitions, `RSS(\beta) := …`, `\sigma(x) := …`, that later lines use, including σ′(x).
  - **Equations, not only identities:** a line set to a value (“−2Xᵀ(y − Xβ) + 2λβ = 0”) is an equation to solve; the next one *follows from it* when it holds wherever the first does (Lemma solves the earlier equation numerically, by Newton's method), and “β̂ = (XᵀX + λI)⁻¹Xᵀy” is reported as *solving line 5 for β*. Inequalities are searched for counterexamples (120 draws).
  - **Check a saved answer:** a ChatGPT answer saved in a note gets **Check n steps**: its displayed equations open as a derivation, so the algebra of an answer is verified on this Mac before you trust it.
  - Derivations are saved with the book, listed in the Lab view, exported as Markdown with each verdict, and copied as an `align*` block. A footnote says what the check is: random draws find wrong steps; agreement is strong evidence, not a proof.
- **Side editors as tabs.** The editor beside the page is now an editor group with its own tabs: several places of the book, Lab notebooks, the dependency map and derivations stay open side by side (at most eight), each with its icon; a derivation's tab shows its failing steps as a badge.
- **Problems panel** (first tab of ⌘J, and ⊗/? counts in the status bar): failing steps of every derivation (errors), passages marked “?” (warnings), exercises attempted but not solved and notes due for recall (information), click one to go there.
- **Line numbers** beside the page, as an editor's gutter (⌃⌘L to hide them): click a number to select its line; type “64:12” in ⌘P to go to line 12 of page 64; the status bar says “p. 64 · Ln 12/41”. Coloured **change bars** mark the lines you highlighted, noted, marked “?” or drew on, your edition as a diff of the book. Papers in two columns get numbers on both sides.
- **Page themes** (⌃⌘T, the layout menu, the palette): Paper, Sepia, and **Night**, light text on a dark page whose colours keep their hue (an orange point stays orange, a blue heading blue), drawn tile by tile from the page. Captures, ChatGPT images and exports keep the original page.
- **Catalog** (activity bar, ⊞): 45 textbooks their authors or publishers give away as PDFs, ESL, ISLR (R and Python), Statistical Learning with Sparsity, Computer Age Statistical Inference, MML, Bishop, Murphy, Boyd–Vandenberghe, MacKay, Axler, Durrett, Vershynin, Sutton–Barto, BDA3 and more, with search and subjects, like an editor's extensions view. **Get** downloads a book from its official address (checked to be a PDF) into your library and opens it. Type an arXiv number or address to open a paper.
- **Find the Paper:** a citation's peek can look the work up (Crossref for the DOI, Semantic Scholar for the abstract and an open PDF, arXiv for preprints; only the reference's printed text is sent, and only when you click), shows each match with how well it fits, and opens the free PDF in a new tab, Go to Definition across documents.

## Earlier: new in 0.9, the structure of a mathematical text, on any book or paper

0.9 reads what a mathematical text is made of, statements, proofs, and which results use which, and makes it navigable as an editor makes code navigable. It was measured on twenty books and papers besides ESL (see VALIDATION), and the analysis now runs in its own process, so a malformed PDF can stop the analysis but never the reader.

- **Results** (activity bar, ✓ seal): every definition, theorem, lemma, proposition, corollary and example by chapter, with its title (“Theorem 3.2 (Gauss–Markov)”, “Jensen’s inequality”), coloured kind badges, how many places use it, its proof, and whether you understood it. Filter by Definitions, Results or Examples; click to go, ⌥-click to open beside the page.
- **Fold proofs (⌥⌘[):** proofs are found from “Proof.” to their end mark (□, ∎, Q.E.D., or amsthm's box drawn with rules) or the next statement. Folding hides a proof behind a paper panel, “Proof of Lemma 2.1 · folded, 12 lines, try it yourself, then click to unfold”, as an editor folds a function body. Each proof also has a fold chevron in the page's gutter. Folded proofs are saved with the book.
- **Hierarchy (⇧⌥⌘H, panel):** an editor's call hierarchy for mathematics. What a statement and its proof use (“by Lemma 2.1”, “(3.4)”, “Theorem 1.3”), and what uses it, each expandable in turn, with counts and “in proof” tags. Uses are read from the references in the text; they are evidence of dependency, not a verified proof graph.
- **Dependency map (⌥⌘G):** the chapter's statements in layers beside the page, prerequisites above what rests on them, arrows from what is used to what uses it; hover a node to follow its links, double-click to see everything it rests on, across chapters. Books without numbered statements (ESL) are mapped by sections.
- **Understood:** mark a statement as understood from Results, the map or its context menu; a green check appears beside it in the page's gutter and the Results view counts your progress. **Add to Review** turns a statement into a recall card (“State Theorem 1.3 and its hypotheses”).
- **Reading ruler (⌥⌘R):** one line of the page highlighted and the rest dimmed, moved with ↓ ↑ or J K (Space jumps three lines), across columns and pages, a proof read one step at a time. Return opens the line's reference; **Why?** asks about the line with its context; the strip shows the statement being read.
- **Notation:** Lemma reads sentences such as “where λ ≥ 0 is a complexity parameter”, “Let X denote the N × p input matrix” or “the hat matrix H”. Rest the pointer on λ, X or β to see what it stands for at that point of the book (the meaning in force, with the page, and every other meaning the book gives the letter). The page inspector gains a **Notation** list, the symbols the page uses and their meanings, and ⌘-click on a symbol shows the same in Look Up.
- **“?” marks (⌥⌘/):** when a line does not make sense, mark it and keep reading. The ruler's line (or the selection) becomes a question note titled “? …” without opening anything; the status bar counts the open marks, and **⌥⌘J** goes back to the first one. A mark leaves the queue once you write its question (it becomes an ordinary question note), resolve it or get it explained.
- **Sticky statement header:** when the head of the theorem or proof you are reading has scrolled away, a header above the page keeps it in view (“Proof of Theorem 3.2 (Gauss–Markov) … from p. 51”), as an editor keeps the enclosing function; click it to go back. The breadcrumb ends with the statement too.
- **Look Up across your library:** ⌘-click or Look up a term and, below the book's own definition, index and your glossary, **In your other books** shows where the other books you have opened define or index it (“Bishop · *ridge regression* · definition”); click to open that book at the page.
- **Anki export:** **File → Export Review Cards for Anki…** writes the review deck as a text file Anki imports (File → Import): the recall prompt with the source image on the front, your answer, reasoning and explanation on the back, tagged and in a deck named after the book.
- **Links:** right-click a statement and choose **Copy Link**: `lemma://open?book=…&ref=Theorem%201.3` opens Lemma at that statement from notes in other apps.
- **Outline for PDFs without one:** papers and older PDFs without bookmarks get an outline from their bold numbered headings, so the breadcrumb, Outline and Results work there too.

The analysis now reads, beyond ESL's conventions: equations numbered “(12)” (papers) and “(A.1)”, labels on the left (`leqno`), captions in the margin (Bishop, Deisenroth et al., MacKay) or without punctuation (“Figure 2.1 The line…”), statement heads in small capitals (“lemma 16.2”) or after the number (“2.35 definition:”), mentions in lower case (“see figure 3.7”), alphabetic citation keys (“[CRT06]”) and citations with pointers (“[3, Theorem 2]”), reference lists and indexes found from their heading when the outline does not name them, a reference list sharing its page with the text, two-column index pages, running heads set lower or larger than ESL's, exercises listed by number under an “Exercises” heading (“2.1 Let C…”, “1.4.1. Show that…”) or set after a solution mark (“◃ Exercise 1.4.”), and statement heads in small capitals followed by their title (“definition 10.1 (Weak learnability)”). Dead references, a mention that names nothing the book defines, are no longer underlined.

## Earlier: new in 0.8, the Lab, hover previews and the page inspector

“Executable equations” and “recreate this figure” become a working notebook beside the page, and the workbench learns what an editor shows without a click.

- **Lab (⌥⌘L, or Lab in the selection bar):** select an equation or a passage and choose Lab, a Python notebook opens beside the page, named after the equation (“Equation (3.44)”), with the passage shown from the original page. Cells run in a local Python process, only when you run them: ⇧↩ runs and moves on, ⌘↩ runs, ⌥↩ runs and adds a cell. Output, the last value (Out[3]), Matplotlib figures and errors with their traceback appear under each cell; variables persist between cells; Tab completes names from the running Python (`np.lin` → `linspace`); ⌘/ comments lines. Text cells take Markdown and LaTeX. A failing cell has **Ask ChatGPT…**, which sends the code, the error and the passage; the answer's corrected cell returns to the open notebook with **Open code in Lab**. The new **Turn into code** help style asks ChatGPT for one runnable NumPy/Matplotlib cell. Notebooks are saved with the book, listed in the Lab view of the activity bar, and exported as Jupyter notebooks (with outputs and figures) and Python scripts. Lemma finds Python itself (python.org, Homebrew, conda, pyenv), preferring one with NumPy and Matplotlib; Settings → Lab chooses another. By default Python runs **gently** (utility priority, two math threads) to keep the Mac cool.
- **Python console (⌃`, or Console in the panel):** compute beside the page, as in an editor's terminal. With a notebook open the console shares its variables; otherwise it uses a scratch session for the book. Syntax-coloured commands, execution counts, results and collapsible tracebacks use the current theme. Return runs complete input (a block runs after an empty line); Run or ⌘↩ runs the input directly. ⇧↩ adds a line, ↑ and ↓ recall earlier input, Tab completes. Clear removes the transcript and keeps variables.
- **Figures in the margin:** hover a figure in a notebook and choose **Pin beside the page**, the plot becomes a margin card beside the passage it illustrates, your own figure next to the book's, and is exported with your notes.
- **Go to Definition for words:** Lemma now reads which words the book sets in italic or bold, from the fonts in the PDF itself, since PDFKit does not report them, and keeps the place each term is introduced, preferring the occurrence with a cue such as “called”, “known as” or “we define”. ⌘-click “deviance” (or Look up) and the popover first shows **Defined in the book**: “*deviance* p. 221, …is sometimes referred to as the deviance”, then the book's index and your glossary; click to go to that sentence (⌥⌘← returns), ⌥-click to open it beside the page. The page inspector lists the terms a page introduces, and Go to (⌘P) finds them by name. Terms are inferred locally from explicit English/French definition cues, titled definition blocks and emphasis corroborated by the book’s index. Italic book titles and rhetorical phrases alone do not count. The analysis refreshes automatically when a previously studied PDF is opened after this update.
- **Hover previews:** rest the pointer on “(3.42)”, “Figure 3.8” or “Hoerl and Kennard (1970)” to see the equation or figure rendered from its page, or the cited entry, with its reference count, no click needed. A click still opens the full peek.
- **Page inspector** (the notebook's new **Page** tab): the page in view, its place in the book and the time you spent on it; the equations and figures defined there, each rendered as printed with its reference count; what the page refers to (open beside the page in one click); the works it cites; the book's own index terms for the page; and your notes, marks and notebooks on it. **Ask about this page** sends the whole page to ChatGPT with its context.
- **Reading time and progress:** Lemma counts reading time on the page in view while its window is in front and you are active. The overview ruler shades the pages you have read, chapters in the outline show a progress ring, the status bar shows today's reading (click for two weeks of bars, the streak and pages read), and the welcome page shows the week across every book. Nothing leaves the Mac.
- **Smaller changes:** outline titles take the spacing printed on their page (“3.4.1 Ridge Regression”, “Support Vector Machines and Flexible Discriminants”); the palette's selection uses the olive accent; the inspector steps aside when a notebook opens in a laptop-sized window and returns when it closes.

## Earlier: new in 0.7, the workbench

Lemma set out to be “VS Code for scientific books”; 0.7 makes the window one, in the native macOS appearance.

- **Activity bar and sidebar views:** Books, Outline (follows your reading position), **Symbols** (every equation, figure, table, algorithm and exercise by chapter, with reference counts and exercise progress), **Index** (the book's own back-of-book index), Search, Marks and Pages.
- **Book tabs and breadcrumbs:** open several books or papers; each keeps its place. The breadcrumb shows Book › Chapter › Section › Subsection; click a crumb to jump to a sibling.
- **Go to (⌘P) and the command palette (⇧⌘P):** type a printed page (“124”), “pdf 143”, “(3.41)”, “fig 3.8”, “ex 2.8”, “3.4.1”, an index term such as “deviance”, or part of a note or section. “>” lists commands, “@” or ⌘T lists symbols. ⌥↩ opens the result beside the page.
- **Side editor (⌘\\):** keep Theorem 4.2 or equation (3.41) open beside the proof you are reading; peeks and results offer Open to the Side.
- **Bottom panel (⌘J):** References lists every place that cites an equation, figure or work (or every page of an index term) with the words around it; Questions and Notes are tables of your work.
